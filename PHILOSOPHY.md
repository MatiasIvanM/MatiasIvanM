# How I work

Short version: I ship results people can rely on, not just features.
This is the longer version, for anyone who wants to know what to expect when working with me.

## 1. Understand the problem behind the request

Most requests arrive as a solution. "Add a button that exports to Excel" is often "the finance team can't reconcile this report." I ask why before I ask how, because the cheapest bug to fix is the one that never gets built.

**In practice:** I want to know who is affected, what they do today, and what changes for them if this works.

## 2. Define "done" before starting

If "done" is a feeling, the work never ends. I write acceptance criteria that can be tested, so everyone agrees on the target before code exists.

**In practice:** a requirement is ready when someone else could write a test for it.

## 3. Make quality visible

Quality is not a phase at the end. Tests, checks, and clear flows are part of the build, and anyone on the team should be able to see their state without asking me.

**In practice:** automated regression checks (Cypress, Jest), a CI pipeline that runs them, and a release that is boring on purpose.

## 4. Stay calm when things go wrong

I spent years as a firefighter. The lessons carried over: prioritize, communicate in short clear sentences, take ownership, and trust the people next to you. Incidents are handled, not panicked over, and afterwards we find out what we missed.

**In practice:** during an incident I state what we know, what we don't, and who is doing what. After it, I write down what changed.

## 5. Build for whoever maintains it next

A solution only I understand is a liability. I prefer readable code, documented decisions, and boring technology where boring is the right call.

**In practice:** every non-obvious decision gets a short note on why it was made and what was rejected.

## 6. Keep the feedback loop short

Small iterations, early demos, and honest status updates. I'd rather show something rough on Tuesday than something polished and wrong on Friday.

---

## What you can expect from me

- Questions early, fewer surprises late.
- Clear communication between technical and non-technical people.
- Work that is tested, documented, and handed over cleanly.
- Straight answers when something is risky, late, or not worth building.
